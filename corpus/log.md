## [2026-09-01] decide | Round 1 of the design grilling settled, 4 decisions recorded in wiki/decisions.md. (1) The estate stays on ONE origin with sub-paths — near-free SSO via a `Path=/` cookie, which also retires atrium's `?token=` workaround; the accepted cost is zero isolation between apps, and subdomains were rejected on the cost of rewriting the master Caddyfile plus atrium's baked-in absolute VITE_API_URL and service-worker cache regex. (2) wzd_auth owns credentials + account identity only; every app keeps its own per-user rows keyed by a stable opaque **subject** — rejected both a central store-of-record for all per-user data (couples six release cycles to one schema) and credentials-only (leaves six answers to "who is this person"). (3) Accounts are for the owner plus known people, no public signup — upholds atrium D30 estate-wide and collides head-on with public-resource-map's shipped registerSchema, logged as a live conflict rather than papered over. (4) Newspapper leaves loopback, which revises `newspapper/corpus/wiki/decisions-security.md` — its public `/uploads/*`, non-revocable 30-day HMAC cookie and IP-keyed lockout now meet real internet traffic. Added **Subject** to the glossary as the identity↔app contract; settled **Profile** as atrium-local. Opened Round 2 (Q5 build-vs-adopt, Q6 session revocability, Q7 credential type, Q8 the prm conflict, Q9 the name).

## [2026-09-01] decide | Round 2 partly settled. Recorded the passwords-only decision (passkeys additive and later, not passkey-only) — the rejected option is written up because it looks strictly more modern and will be proposed again, and it fails specifically on atrium D35's shared household tablet, passkeys being per-device credentials. Q5 (build rather than adopt) treated as answered by implication, since choosing a token design and refresh policy is only a decision one gets to make when building; being confirmed explicitly. Two contradictions surfaced rather than papered over and carried into Round 3: (a) "JWTs plus a table of revoked JWTs" — a per-request denylist check pays the lookup a JWT exists to avoid, so it is opaque sessions with extra steps; the resolving shape is a short-lived access JWT never checked against a denylist plus a long-lived opaque refresh token that is a row, where revoking is deleting the row and no denylist exists. (b) prm joining wzd_auth collides head-on with the no-public-signup decision, since prm ships public self-registration. Also opened: token signing (shared HMAC lets any app mint tokens for all six; asymmetric only verifies), and the canonical identifier conflict — atrium and newspapper key on username, prm on email plus emailVerified.

## [2026-09-01] decide | Round 3 settled; the design has a shape. Named the service **Ward** (a warding enchantment on a threshold — access control, puns on "warden", keeps the Atrium/Citadel/Hollow architectural streak); retired `wzd_auth`, which named the estate's identity service after newspapper's markup file extension. Split the decisions page: identity/scope/naming stay in wiki/decisions.md, session mechanics move to wiki/decisions-tokens.md. Locked the token shape after researching current practice — short-lived signed access JWT + opaque **rotating** refresh row with reuse detection killing the family, and **no denylist**: rejected the originally-requested revoked-JWT table because a per-request denylist pays the very lookup a JWT exists to avoid, and once the revocable thing is a row, "revoked" is its absence. Recorded OWASP's gotcha in case a denylist ever returns — key it on `jti`, never the raw token or its hash, which JWT malleability walks straight past. Adopted the owner's introspection-with-cache proposal over my own recommendation: the sources back it for the multi-service case, since revoking becomes one operation rather than six lookups and six cache-invalidation problems, and Ward shares a box with all six apps so it is a loopback call. Permissions deliberately excluded from token claims — a token minted before a grant changed would carry stale authority for its full lifetime. Signing is EdDSA held by Ward alone; rejected shared HMAC, which would let any one compromised app mint identity for all six. Username chosen as canonical identifier over email (owner's call, against my recommendation) — carried the consequence forward rather than dropping it: prm keys on email, so its rows do not migrate mechanically and recovery has no channel yet. **Q12 revised two Round 1 decisions**: prm joins Ward, public registration is allowed, and the security boundary moves from registration to authorization — a `(subject, app, role)` grant, with role strings stored opaquely so Ward never needs redeploying when an app grows a capability. Opened Round 4 on the tail that created: grant cardinality, which apps accept signup, whether email is collected and verified, and the two lifetime numbers.

## [2026-09-01] decide | Round 4 settled; the design grilling is complete at 13 decisions. A grant carries a **set** of roles, not one — rejected single-role (simpler, matches prm today) because a set costs nothing now and avoids a migration the first time any of six apps needs two at once. Registration is open **per app via a flag, closed by default** — rejected Ward-level signup because it fails in the wrong direction, leaving a new app reachable by strangers until someone remembers to close it; the flag fails safe. Email is **optional, verified only on the public path** — owner-issued accounts skip it entirely, which puts the cost exactly where the strangers are; two costs accepted explicitly, a mail sender plus verification flow now in scope (the largest single chunk of build cost here), and owner-issued accounts having no recovery path at all. Lifetimes: 15-minute access token, 30-second introspection cache, 30-day rotating refresh — recorded that the 30 seconds is the number that matters, since apps introspect on every request, and that logout is near-instant in practice because it clears the cookie, leaving the window exposed only to an already-stolen token. Split the decisions page a second time (it had reached 190 of 200 lines): foundational scope stays in decisions.md, accounts/registration/grants move to decisions-accounts.md, tokens stay in decisions-tokens.md. One question left open and it is a fact rather than a preference — whether prm has live accounts that must survive, which is all that blocks a migration plan, since prm keys on email and every existing row would need a username that does not exist yet.

## [2026-09-01] decide | Owner chose to prune rather than migrate: every existing credential store is emptied and accounts are recreated in Ward. Closes Q19 outright — prm's email-keyed rows need no username invented because they do not survive. Rejected migrating atrium's scrypt hashes, which would have worked, as not worth the code for a population of the owner plus a few known people. **Caught a destructive failure mode while scoping it**, recorded rather than discovered later: in atrium `profiles.user_id` references `users` ON DELETE CASCADE and `reading_progress.profile_id` / `latex_projects.profile_id` cascade from `profiles` in turn, so emptying `users` destroys reading progress and LaTeX projects; `notes.profile_id` and `note_folders.profile_id` are ON DELETE RESTRICT and would block the delete instead — an accidental safety net. Authored content is not auth data and is out of scope for the prune; where exactly the boundary falls is Q20 and nothing is to be deleted until it is answered. Also recorded the first-boot admin: WARD_ADMIN_USERNAME / WARD_ADMIN_PASSWORD seed the first account only when the users table is empty, newspapper's existing pattern — the first-boot-only semantics being the surprising half, since re-reading every boot would make .env the permanent source of truth for a live credential and turn a password change into a redeploy. A UI is newly in scope; its shape (one login page or six, console contents, self-service, whether ward:admin is just another grant) is Round 5.

## [2026-09-01] decide | Round 5 settled; 17 decisions. **Prune takes everything** (Q20) — the owner was shown exactly what cascades and chose the clean slate, so atrium's profiles, reading progress, notes and LaTeX projects go, along with prm's favorites, notifications and tokens; nothing is re-anchored because nothing survives. Verified prm's shape while scoping it: Drizzle with `ON DELETE no action` on every FK, so a `user` delete fails until its dependents go first, and all of its user-scoped tables are derived data with no authored content to lose — also noted that prm already ships `reset_token` and `verification_token`, so the email machinery Q17 put in scope can be cribbed rather than invented. Cutover brief must take a database copy first; atrium's own `backup-pre-brief35-*` directory is the precedent. **One central login page** at `/ward/login` with `?next=` (Q21) — rejected per-app forms because Ward exists to delete six copies of auth and per-app forms keep six copies of the form, where lockout and verification states get subtly wrong six ways; the accepted cost is that atrium's Reading Room and newspapper's Mechanical both hand their sign-in moment to a third visual identity. **UI is console plus minimal self-service** (Q22), self-service earning its place because owner-issued accounts have no verified email and therefore no recovery channel, leaving "sign out my other devices" as the only self-serve response to a suspected theft. **Superuser is environment-only with no account row** (Q23) — this REVISES the first-boot-admin decision recorded earlier the same day; rejected both seeding a first admin row (an ordinary account can be locked out, revoked or deleted, which is what a recovery mechanism must not be) and `ward:admin` as a grant (elegant, but leaves the estate one bad revoke from nobody being able to grant anything). The trade accepted explicitly: a live credential now sits in plaintext on disk permanently, defensible as break-glass rather than a daily driver, and the only thing that still works when the database is empty or its last admin is gone.

## [2026-09-01] decide | Round 6 settled; the design grilling is complete at 20 decisions across four pages. Split administration onto its own page, wiki/decisions-admin.md, because two privileged identities now exist and conflating them is the mistake most worth preventing. **The superuser reaches the console and nothing else** (Q24) — needing no enforcement code, since access is a grant and the superuser has none; its session is a distinct console session and never a JWT. Rejected a reserved sentinel subject, which works only while all six apps remember to reject it — it fails on discipline, where a non-JWT session cannot accidentally satisfy an app's ordinary auth check at all. Rejected a superuser bypass whose only effect would be letting a break-glass credential read the library and notes. **The apps are run by an ordinary owner account, not the superuser** (Q25, the owner's own framing) — this is the half that makes the superuser decision safe, since without a daily driver the break-glass credential inevitably becomes the everyday login, which is precisely what a credential with no revocation, rotation or audit trail must never become. Its grants are explicit, one per app, no wildcard — a `*:admin` would make a newly-added app reachable the moment it exists, contradicting the fails-safe property the per-app registration flag was chosen for. **Console access cannot be delegated** (Q26), recorded as a limit rather than an oversight: one credential, one administrator, correct for one operator and wrong the moment there are two, at which point `ward:admin` with a refuse-to-remove-the-last-one guard is the design set aside. Added **superuser** and **owner account** to the glossary as explicitly non-interchangeable terms. open-questions.md is now empty, which is the goal state.

Built by five parallel agents against controller-pinned contracts, then three scoped review finders and one fix round. Opened **wiki/decisions-implementation.md** as a fifth decisions page — decisions.md was at 144 of the 200-line cap and every remaining brief lands engineering calls, so splitting now beats splitting mid-build. Recorded there: better-sqlite3 over Knex (atrium's D47 records three correctness properties that broke when statements could interleave; Ward's hot path is session lookup under concurrency, so synchronous is the mechanism rather than a limitation), static-import migrations over a directory scan (a scan finds a different set of files depending on whether the process started from `.ts` under tsx or `.js` under `dist/`), scrypt over argon2id (zero native dependencies — a native build failing on a Node upgrade takes the estate's login with it — and the full prune means no hash constrains the choice; revisit at volume, since rehash-on-next-login is always available), and loopback binding (atrium binds `0.0.0.0` and rests "Caddy is the only way in" on the firewall alone). **Review caught two Criticals the gates could not.** (1) `.gitignore` never covered the Ed25519 signing key at the path `.env.example` itself suggests, so one `git add -A` would have committed the estate's only signing key — recovery being rotation across all six apps plus a history scrub. Found independently by two finders. (2) `db.close()` was never called on shutdown, so SQLite never ran its close-time WAL checkpoint: measured `ward.db` at 4096 bytes with **zero tables** after a full boot→serve→SIGTERM cycle, which is every pm2 restart and every deploy. Since `.env.example` correctly tells the operator that file "is the only copy of who anybody is", brief 11's backup would have copied an empty database and reported success. Recorded as a decision rather than a bug fix, because the tempting cleanup — "`process.exit` closes everything anyway" — is wrong for a reason nothing in the code makes visible. Also fixed: `openDatabase` was untestable by construction (the eager singleton meant importing it evaluated config.ts and could `process.exit`, which would have broken brief 01's first test — `getDb()` is now async precisely because only a *dynamic* import genuinely defers that), the runner could not detect a renamed migration, and the migration-author contract stated a false constraint about nested transactions. **Two deviations recorded rather than hidden:** the repo directory is still `wzd_auth` (the owner deferred the rename because it moves the directory their session is open in; `package.json` says `ward` and nothing else references the old name), and there is no test framework, so the loopback and fail-closed properties are hand-verified with nothing guarding them against regression — brief 01 is the place to land a runner. `typescript` pinned to 6.0.3 rather than the latest 7.0.2, because `typescript-eslint` peer-requires `<6.1.0`; revisit when that ecosystem catches up to the TS 7 native port.
