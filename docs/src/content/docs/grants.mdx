---
title: Grants — the security boundary
description: Anyone may hold a Ward account; a (subject, app, role) grant is what lets them reach anything. The single largest change the design went through.
---

A grant is a row:

```
(subject, app_slug, role)
```

"This person may use this app in this capacity." It is the estate's **actual
security boundary**, and arriving at that was the biggest change the design went
through.

## Registration used to be the boundary

The original decision was operator-seeded accounts and no public signup:
existing meant permitted.

Then `public-resource-map` turned out to already have public self-registration,
roles, and email verification — a live contradiction with a deployed app on the
other side of it. Resolving it moved the boundary: **anyone may register; a
grant is what confers access.**

That revised two earlier decisions and pulled public signup, with its abuse and
recovery costs, into scope. It is the right shape and it is also the single
largest source of remaining work. See
[decisions-accounts](/wiki/decisions-accounts/).

## What registering gets you

Almost nothing. An account, a subject, and whatever `baseline_role` the app you
registered at chooses to grant — which may be nothing at all.

Public registration is **per app**, a flag on the `apps` row, and the schema
enforces the pairing:

```sql
CHECK (public_registration = 0 OR baseline_role IS NOT NULL)
```

An app cannot open signup without saying what a new registrant becomes. Leaving
that unstated is the mistake this constraint exists to prevent — an open door
onto an undefined authority.

Today `prm` is the only app with the flag on.

## Roles are opaque to Ward

Ward stores role strings and never interprets them. `reader`, `admin`,
`contributor` mean whatever the app that defined them says they mean.

This is a real boundary, not a convenience. Ward answering "is this person an
admin?" would put every app's authorization model inside the identity service,
and the six apps here do not agree on one. Ward answers "which roles does this
person hold for your app?" and the app decides what follows.

For consumers this has a concrete rule: **test membership, never equality.**

```ts
// Right
if (hasGrant(session.grants, 'atrium', 'admin')) { … }

// Wrong — breaks the moment anyone holds two roles
if (session.grants.atrium?.[0] === 'admin') { … }
```

## Slugs are pinned to lower case

```sql
CHECK (slug <> '' AND slug = lower(slug))
```

Slugs appear in grant rows, in URLs, and in six apps' configuration. Pinning the
case in the schema stops `Atrium` and `atrium` becoming two apps that look like
one — a class of bug that is invisible in the console and total in effect.

## Cascade, and what it means for the cutover

```sql
subject  REFERENCES users (subject) ON DELETE CASCADE
app_slug REFERENCES apps  (slug)    ON DELETE CASCADE
```

Deleting an account takes its grants with it. Deleting an app takes every grant
into it.

That is correct, and it is also why the cutover is destructive by decision: the
migration to Ward prunes every account in the estate and recreates them, taking
atrium's profiles and reading progress, its notes and LaTeX projects, and prm's
favorites and notifications with them. The owner was shown the cascade and chose
a clean slate. See [Status](/wiki/status/) — that brief has not run.

## Two privileged identities, not interchangeable

| | Superuser | Owner account |
|---|---|---|
| Where it lives | environment variables only | an ordinary row, like anyone else |
| Can reach | Ward's admin console, and nothing else | the apps, via explicit admin grants |
| Rotation | requires a redeploy | a password change |
| Purpose | break glass | daily administration |

The superuser has no row, no subject and no grants, so it cannot be granted
access to an app even by mistake. There is deliberately **no way to delegate
console access** — the console is the one credential that cannot be revoked
without a redeploy, and handing it out would multiply that.

Full reasoning in [decisions-admin](/wiki/decisions-admin/), which also explains
why "admin" unqualified is a word to avoid here.

## The console

`/ward/console` — superuser only, and visibly not the ordinary login page.

That is a safety property rather than a style choice: every route on it changes
authority, and someone reaching for break-glass should be able to tell they
have. The UI re-points its design tokens to a dark palette on a wrapper so the
whole surface reads as a different building.
